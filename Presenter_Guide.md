## 6. Likely questions

All twelve are also in the Rehearsal Coach's Q&A fire drill, with model answers.

**Why do the deep perforating arteries bleed and not the big ones?** They branch at right angles straight off large, high-pressure arteries, so they get little pressure buffering. Years of hypertension leave a thick but brittle wall.

**Are Charcot–Bouchard microaneurysms real?** They were described in 1868. Later work suggests some were artefacts, so their role is still debated.

**How do you confirm CAA?** Definite CAA needs pathology: amyloid shows apple-green birefringence on Congo red. In life, the Boston criteria use MRI (lobar microbleeds and superficial siderosis). Version 2.0 (2022) added white-matter markers.

**Why are anticoagulant bleeds worse?** The blood can't clot well, so the bleed keeps growing for longer. In SMASH-U they were the largest bleeds, and 54% of those patients were dead at three months.

**Is lowering blood pressure risky for the tissue around the clot?** No. PET showed the tissue is resting, not starving (Zazulia 2001). In ICH ADAPT (2013), lowering systolic pressure below 150 didn't reduce flow. INTERACT2 (2013) found intensive lowering to below 140 was safe.

**What is the spot sign?** Contrast leaking into the clot on CT angiography, which means the bleed is still active. It predicts growth (PREDICT, 2012).

**How is haemorrhagic transformation different?** The infarct comes first; the damaged tissue then bleeds, often after reperfusion. In primary ICH, a diseased small vessel ruptures.

**Why do patients worsen on days 3 to 5?** Oedema is still building and iron is being released. Hydrocephalus can develop, and so can pneumonia or seizures.

**Is there a drug for secondary injury?** Not yet. The i-DEF trial of deferoxamine (2019) showed no meaningful benefit at 90 days.

**When should rehabilitation start?** Follow the medical team's parameters and be most watchful in the first 24 to 72 hours. AVERT (2015) found very early, high-dose mobilisation within 24 hours reduced good outcomes, and it included some ICH patients. Once the patient is stable, use short, frequent sessions with a neuro check first.

**Is ICH more common in some settings?** Yes. It makes up about 28% of strokes worldwide, and incidence is highest in lower-middle-income countries (Wolsink 2024). Uncontrolled hypertension is the main driver.

**What's new since 2014?** Go to hidden slide 31.
